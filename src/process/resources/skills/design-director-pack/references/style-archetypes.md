# Design Style Archetypes

Use these archetypes as first-party visual directions. They are distilled from reviewed DESIGN.md references and should be treated as reusable design-system lenses, not as brand copies.

## 1. `precision-mono`

Best for:

- developer products
- infrastructure tools
- technical dashboards
- products that need precision, speed, and trust through restraint

Core signals:

- black, white, near-black, and tightly controlled accent usage
- compressed headings, disciplined typography, exact spacing
- borders, rings, or shadow-as-border instead of decorative flourish
- high information confidence, low decorative noise

Common failure modes:

- becoming sterile or hostile
- over-compressing type without enough whitespace
- using monochrome without a clear hierarchy

## 2. `warm-editorial`

Best for:

- workspace tools
- writing and knowledge products
- collaborative products that should feel calm and intelligent

Core signals:

- warm neutrals instead of cold grayscale
- editorial pacing and softer surfaces
- content-first layout, comfortable reading rhythm
- approachable minimalism with restrained accent use

Common failure modes:

- becoming bland or sleepy
- losing product hierarchy because everything is equally soft
- confusing "warm" with "low contrast"

## 3. `vibrant-tooling`

Best for:

- creative tools
- collaboration products
- design-forward SaaS that still needs operational clarity

Core signals:

- structural UI remains disciplined while content areas carry energy
- bold accent fields, colorful showcases, or vivid motion moments
- playful but controlled component geometry
- clear split between chrome and expressive content

Common failure modes:

- color everywhere with no control surface
- decorative gradients replacing true emphasis
- landing-page energy leaking into dense product surfaces

## Choosing between them

Ask:

- Should the product feel precise, calm, or energetic first?
- Is the main trust source rigor, readability, or creative confidence?
- Is this a marketing surface, a working surface, or a hybrid?

If more than one archetype fits:

- choose one primary archetype
- allow one secondary accent influence
- never mix three equal archetypes in the same surface
