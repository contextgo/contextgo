# Startup Strategic Diagnosis

Use this skill when the startup needs a hard strategic read, not just descriptive market commentary.

## Use when

- The user wants to pressure-test a startup concept.
- Market timing or competitive context is still unclear.
- A SWOT-style diagnosis is needed before choosing a strategy.

## Do not use when

- The task is a narrow execution plan.
- The user only wants a polished narrative without strategic judgment.

## Diagnosis failures to avoid

- confusing market size with startup viability
- treating every competitor as equally relevant
- listing strengths and weaknesses with no strategic implication
- ignoring why now
- skipping the assumptions that must be true for the plan to work

## Core lenses

### 1. Market timing

Ask:

- why this problem matters now
- what trend, shift, or trigger makes the opportunity more real today than before

### 2. Alternative landscape

Map:

- direct competitors
- indirect alternatives
- "do nothing" behavior

### 3. SWOT

Be explicit about:

- strengths
- weaknesses
- opportunities
- threats

Tie the matrix back to the startup, not to generic market facts.

### 4. Strategic pressure points

Look for:

- distribution difficulty
- switching friction
- trust gaps
- platform dependence
- resource constraints
- defensibility weakness

### 5. Must-be-true assumptions

Name the few assumptions that would materially break the thesis if false.

## Output format

Return:

### 1. Strategic diagnosis

- what is really going on
- why now may or may not be credible

### 2. Alternative landscape

- current alternatives
- what they do well
- where they leave gaps

### 3. SWOT matrix

- strengths
- weaknesses
- opportunities
- threats

### 4. Strategic implications

- what this means for focus
- what this means for sequencing
- what this means for defensibility

### 5. Must-be-true assumptions and next tests
